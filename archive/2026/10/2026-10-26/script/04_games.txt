Now, something you asked for: retro gaming projects. And there's been a flood of them. The big trend is "recomp", short for recompilation. Fans pull an old game apart into readable code, then rebuild it to run natively on a modern computer, rather than emulating the old console. In the last fortnight alone we've had Project Midas, a PC version of GoldenEye built from the Nintendo sixty four decompilation; native PC versions of Lost Odyssey and Crackdown from the Xbox three sixty; all four classic Twisted Metal games in one launcher; a God of War recompilation that builds the game from your own PlayStation two disc; and a new version of Eternal Darkness with fresh camera options. A lot of these were made with help from A I coding tools, and that's caused a proper argument. The emulation community on Reddit now asks for an "A I-assisted" label, the decomp dot dev site switched off new sign-ups because of A I-driven abuse, and one team announced its Super Smash Bros. port as deliberately "human-made".

Here's the twist: Microsoft has noticed. It's set up a small team called Xbox Labs to explore decompilation and recompilation, the exact technique behind those fan ports. [happy] Which brings us nicely to your Retro Game of the Week. After a Super Nintendo classic last week, this week it's a Mega Drive game, and I picked it with your own game in mind. It's Beyond Oasis, called The Story of Thor in Europe. It came out in Japan at the end of 1994 and in the West in 1995, made by a studio called Ancient, which was run by Yuzo Koshiro, the composer behind Streets of Rage, with art by his sister, Ayano.

Why this one? You loved A Link to the Past's top-down adventuring, and Secret of Mana is the spirit of your own game. Beyond Oasis sits right between them. It's a real-time, top-down action role-playing game, but the combat borrows from fighting games: you dash, jump kick, crouch and pull off special moves with button combinations. Prince Ali has an unbreakable dagger, plus swords, crossbows and bombs that wear out after so many uses. But the thing I really want you to see is the spirits. Ali wears a golden armlet, and he summons four elemental spirits by aiming it at things in the world. Point it at water and you get Dytto, a water spirit who heals and puts out fires. Fire calls up Efreet. Shadows call Shade, and plants call a spirit called Bow. They fight beside you and solve puzzles. For a man designing a farming action game, a system where the world itself is your summoning circle is a gold mine. Water from your well, fire from your forge.

What to look out for: the huge, full-screen bosses, the gorgeous animation, and Koshiro's soundtrack. It won a magazine's action R P G of the year back in 1995. How long? Players report anything from under ten hours to the high teens, so it's a comfortable week. And the easy legal way to play it: it's in the Sega Mega Drive app on Nintendo Switch Online, if you've got the Expansion Pack, listed as Beyond Oasis, The Story of Thor. It's also on the Mega Drive Mini and in Sega's Genesis Classics collection. Check it's still there before you settle in.
