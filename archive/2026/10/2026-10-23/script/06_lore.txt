[happy] Deep dive time. A few days ago we did the fall of Arthas, and you gave the lore segment top marks, so today I'm going back to Warcraft for the other great tragic figure of that world. Illidan Stormrage. The Betrayer. The man with the blindfold and the very complicated family.

Illidan is a night elf, and his story starts ten thousand years before the first Warcraft game, with his twin brother, Malfurion. Two brothers, very different. Malfurion becomes a druid, patient, connected to nature. Illidan is drawn to arcane magic: fast, brilliant, ambitious, and hungry for power. And both of them love the same woman, the priestess Tyrande Whisperwind. She chooses Malfurion. Keep that in mind, because almost everything Illidan does for the next ten thousand years has a little of that rejection in it.

Then comes the War of the Ancients. The night elf queen, Azshara, and her court of Highborne sorcerers are drawing more and more power from the Well of Eternity, a lake of pure magic, and they open a doorway for the Burning Legion, an army of demons led by the fallen titan Sargeras. Illidan plays a dangerous double game: he goes to the demons, and Sargeras gives him a gift. Sargeras burns out his eyes and gives him a new sight that sees magic itself, along with glowing tattoos. That's where the blindfold comes from. He isn't blind. He sees more than anyone else in the room. He just can't see the way we do.

The war ends in catastrophe. The Well of Eternity implodes and {tears|tairs} the continent apart, an event called the Sundering. And here's the moment that defines Illidan. He had secretly saved vials of the Well's water, and he pours them into a lake on top of Mount Hyjal, creating a new Well of Eternity. To him, it's preserving magic for his people. To his brother, it's exactly the kind of reckless power-grab that just broke the world. So Malfurion has him imprisoned underground, for ten thousand years, guarded by a warden named Maiev Shadowsong.

[dark] Ten thousand years in a cell. [beat] Think about the small talk. By year forty you've told every story you know. By year two thousand you've named every crack in the ceiling. By year nine thousand, Maiev is still standing outside the door, and she has never once laughed at a joke.

When Warcraft three arrives in 2002, Tyrande sets him free to help fight the undead Scourge. And Illidan, being Illidan, takes the fast route to power: he consumes the Skull of Gul'dan, a demonic artefact, and becomes half demon, with horns and wings. He uses that power to kill a demon lord, which really does help. His brother's response is to banish him anyway. You can see why Illidan keeps feeling unappreciated.

In the expansion, The Frozen Throne, the demon lord Kil'jaeden gives Illidan a job: destroy the Lich King. Illidan fails, flees to Outland, the shattered world of the orcs, and builds his own little empire with blood elves and the serpent-like naga. And then he meets Arthas, at the top of Icecrown, and loses. Two fallen heroes, one sword fight, and the one with the better soundtrack wins.

That sets up The Burning Crusade in 2007, where Illidan is the final boss of the Black Temple raid. When you walk into his chamber, he says the line that became one of the most quoted in all of World of Warcraft: "You are not prepared!" Players beat him, and Maiev gets her revenge. For years, that was the end of his story.

[happy] Except it wasn't. In Legion, in 2016, Blizzard brought him back. Demon hunters became a playable class, Illidan's own followers, blindfolds and all. And the big reveal was that he'd been right about a lot of things: he'd spent ten thousand years understanding the Burning Legion better than anyone, and he'd been preparing to fight it the whole time, in his own unbearable way. Fans had been saying "Illidan was right" for years as a half-joke. Legion made it canon-ish. At the end of that story, he chooses to stay behind and stand guard over the imprisoned Sargeras, forever. The brother who wanted everything ends up choosing duty.

That's why he works. He's not a villain who turns good, and he's not a hero who falls. He's someone who's always certain he knows best, and is right just often enough to make everyone furious.
