And now, my favourite new part of the show. It's the first Tuesday with our Actress of the Month.

For October, I've picked Sigourney Weaver.

Why her? Because she changed what a lead in a science fiction horror film could be. In nineteen seventy nine, a relatively unknown stage actress, trained at Stanford and the Yale School of Drama, was cast as Ellen Ripley in Ridley Scott's Alien. Ripley was written so that the character could be played by a man or a woman, and Weaver made her practical, stubborn, frightened and fierce all at once. She became the survivor everyone remembers, and she played Ripley across four films. She's also brilliant in comedy, in Ghostbusters and later in Galaxy Quest, and in drama, but for this show, she's sci-fi horror royalty. And there's a lovely link today, because Ridley Scott's Alien is our director deep dive later on, and yesterday we went deep on James Cameron's Terminator two.

Which brings me to this week's film recommendation: Aliens, from nineteen eighty six, directed by James Cameron.

It picks up fifty seven years after the first film. Ripley is found drifting in space, nobody believes her story, and the colony on the moon where her crew found the alien egg has gone silent. She goes back with a unit of colonial marines, and what follows is a masterclass in tension that turns into one of the great action films. Cameron swaps the haunted house of the first film for a war movie, but keeps the dread.

Why it's worth your time: Weaver's performance earned her an Academy Award nomination for best actress, which was almost unheard of for a science fiction action film at the time. Her relationship with the young survivor Newt gives the film a real heart, and the finale, Ripley versus the alien queen, is one of the best showdowns ever put on film.

What to look out for: the motion tracker. Cameron uses that little beeping sound to wind the tension up long before you see anything. Watch how he cuts the marines' confidence down piece by piece. And watch the queen sequence closely, because a huge amount of that is practical, from Stan Winston's team, which ties in nicely with the creature effects we're talking about later today.

Where to find it: JustWatch lists Aliens on Disney Plus in Australia, in four K, and it's also available to buy on Apple TV and Amazon, but check on the day because catalogues change. For a physical copy, there's the twenty twenty four four K Ultimate Collector's Edition, which has both the theatrical cut and the longer nineteen ninety special edition. A fair warning, though: that four K was cleaned up with a machine learning process, and many fans and reviewers feel the grain has been scrubbed away and it looks a little too smooth and modern. So, enjoy it, but if you love film grain, go in with eyes open. My suggestion for a first watch is the special edition. It's longer, but it adds some lovely character moments.
