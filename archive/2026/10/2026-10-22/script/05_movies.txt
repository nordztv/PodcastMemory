Aliens was made in nineteen eighty five, mainly at Pinewood Studios outside London, for about eighteen and a half million dollars, on a seventy five day shoot. James Cameron was thirty one, coming off The Terminator, and he wanted to do something different from Ridley Scott's original. Not one monster in the dark, but an army of them. Not a haunted house, but a war film.

The colony's processing station, the place with the alien hive, was filmed in a real decommissioned power station in west London, Acton Lane. It had to be professionally cleaned of decaying asbestos first, which took three weeks. And those sets were later reused by Tim Burton for Batman.

The tough part was the crew. Cameron and his producer, Gale Anne Hurd, were young Americans running a very traditional British crew, and the two sides did not get on. The crew stopped for tea breaks every day, and Cameron saw them as wasted shooting time. When Cameron and Hurd fired the first assistant director, the crew walked out in the middle of the day. It took a long meeting to settle it: Cameron agreed to respect tea time, and the crew agreed to back him. The cinematographer, Dick Bush, was also replaced by Adrian Biddle, after insisting on lighting the hive brightly when Cameron wanted it dark. And the original Hicks was James Remar, who left early in the shoot. Michael Biehn stepped in.

Then there's the Queen. Stan Winston's team built her as a fourteen foot puppet out of lightweight foam. Two people sat inside the torso working the arms, rods moved the legs, the tail was whipped about by hand on wires, and the head was moved with hydraulics. The whole thing hung from a crane, hidden by steam, slime, smoke and very careful lighting. One of Winston's crew said there were "nine of us on her tail, her head, the cable, her hands, the feet". The first test version was made of plastic bags hanging from a crane. And then that Power Loader fight. The loader wasn't powered at all. A stuntman stood behind it, dressed in black, working it by hand.

The music was a scramble too. James Horner expected six weeks to write the score. He got about three, because Cameron was still shooting and cutting. Parts of the Queen battle were written overnight.

And it paid off. Aliens took about eighty five million dollars in America, and got seven Oscar nominations, winning two, for sound effects editing and visual effects. And the big one: Sigourney Weaver was nominated for Best Actress. A Best Actress nomination for a science fiction action film, in nineteen eighty six. That just didn't happen. Ripley in Aliens is a mother, a soldier and a survivor at the same time, and Weaver made people take all of it seriously.
