Game tech now, and today's topic comes straight out of your GO-DOT project, and out of this week's retro game. It's Y sorting. Or in plain words: how a top down game decides who's standing in front of whom.

Let's start with the basics. A two D game is really a stack of flat pictures, drawn one after another onto the screen. Whatever gets drawn last ends up on top. Programmers call this the painter's algorithm, because it's how a painter works. Paint the sky first, then the mountains, then the trees, then the person standing in front of the trees.

In a side scrolling game, that's easy. The background goes at the back, the character goes at the front, done. But in a top down game, like A Link to the Past or Secret of Mana, there's a problem. The camera looks down at the world at an angle. So when your hero walks behind a tree, the tree should cover the hero. But when your hero walks in front of the tree, the hero should cover the tree. The same two objects need to swap order depending on where they are.

The trick is beautifully simple. Things that are higher up on the screen are further away, and things lower down on the screen are closer to you. So you sort everything by its vertical position, its Y coordinate. Draw the things with a smaller Y first, and the things with a bigger Y last. Hence, Y sorting.

There's one detail that makes or breaks it, though, and it's where the sorting point is. Imagine the tree's sorting point is in the middle of its trunk, and your hero's is in the middle of his chest. Your hero walks up behind the tree, but his chest is still lower on screen than the tree's middle, so he gets drawn on top of it, and now he looks like he's floating in front of a tree he should be hiding behind. It looks wrong straight away. The fix is to put the sorting point at the feet. Where the character touches the ground, and where the tree's trunk meets the ground. Because in a top down world, the feet are what tell your brain where something really is.

Now in GO-DOT four, here's how it works. In GO-DOT three, there used to be a special node called Y Sort. In GO-DOT four that node is gone, and instead, every two D node has a property called Y Sort Enabled. You turn that on for the parent node that holds your characters, props and trees, and GO-DOT sorts all of its children by their Y position automatically, every frame.

So, a practical setup for your game. Make a node, call it something like World, and switch on Y Sort Enabled. Put your player, your enemies, your N P Cs and your trees and rocks under it as children. Then, for each of those scenes, make sure the node's origin, its zero point, sits at the feet. The easiest way is to move the sprite up, so its bottom edge sits on the origin. In the editor you'll see the little crosshair at the base of the character. That's what you want.

Tile maps are the other half. In GO-DOT four, tile map layers can also have Y sort enabled, and each tile in your tile set has a setting for its Y sort origin, so a tall tree tile can sort from the base of its trunk. That way, you can paint your forests with the tile map and still have your hero walk behind them properly.

Two common traps. First, if you have Y sort on but things still look wrong, check you don't have different Z index values fighting each other. The Z index is like a separate layer number, and it wins over the Y sort. Use Z index for whole layers, like the ground, and things that should always float above everything, like a roof or a bird. Use Y sorting for everything at character height. Second, if a child node has its own children, like a sword sprite attached to the player, make sure it's sorting with its parent and not separately, or you'll get the classic glitch where the sword slices through the tree but the arm holding it goes behind. Which, honestly, is a very cursed look. A floating sword hacking at a tree while the hero's arm is trapped inside the trunk, slowly being absorbed into the wood, as the tree whispers welcome home.

So here's your practical tip for the week. Open your project, make a test scene with your player and three trees, turn on Y Sort Enabled on the parent, and walk in circles around the trees. If your hero always disappears and reappears at exactly the right moment, you're done. And then, when you're playing A Link to the Past this week, watch Link walk around pots, trees and walls, and notice how invisible it all is. When Y sorting is done right, nobody ever notices it. That's the goal.
