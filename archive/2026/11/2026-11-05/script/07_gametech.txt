[warm] Interaction. Pressing a button next to something, and having the world respond. Pulling a crop, opening a door, talking to Chen, feeding the nurikabe. In a farming game it's almost everything. And it's the first step of tomorrow's radish build-along, because before a radish monster can burst out of the ground, you need to be able to pull a radish.

[curious] First, the idea, with an everyday picture. Think about automatic doors at the shops. You don't press a button to open them. The door has a sensor, a little invisible zone in front of it, and when you walk into the zone, it notices you. Godot has exactly that. It's called an Area2D.

[calm] An Area2D is an invisible zone. It doesn't block anything, and nothing bumps into it. It just notices. Give it a CollisionShape2D child, to say how big the zone is, and it will tell you when something enters or leaves. It does that with signals: body entered, when a physics body like your farmer walks in, and body exited, when they leave. There's also area entered and area exited, for when two zones overlap.

[thoughtful] Now, there are two ways to set this up, and the choice matters. You can put a zone on every object: every radish, every door, every stall, each one waiting for the farmer to walk in. Or you can put one zone on the farmer: a little "reach" zone just in front of them, and let it find whatever's there. [happy] For a farming game, the second way is usually nicer. The farmer reaches, and whatever is in reach can be used. It's one place to write the logic, and it works the same for a radish as for a door.

[curious] So how does the farmer know what it's touching? Groups. In Godot, you can add any node to a named group, like a label on a box. Put your radish in a group called "interactable". Put the door in it too, and Chen's stall. Then, when the player presses the button, the reach zone asks: what areas am I overlapping? That's a function called get overlapping areas. And for each one, is it in the "interactable" group? If yes, tell it to interact.

[calm] The button itself goes in the Input Map, just like yesterday's movement. Add an action called "interact", and give it E, or the space bar, and a button on the controller. Then, in your player script, check Input dot is action just pressed, interact. The "just" is important: it's true only on the frame you press it, not every frame you hold it down. Otherwise, holding the button would pull sixty radishes a second. [sly] Which, with a radish monster in the game... [punch] is a very quick way to meet Sir.

[curious] Now, the one gotcha that catches everyone. Collision layers and masks. [thoughtful] Think of layers as what something is, and masks as what it's looking for. Your farmer might be on layer one. Your interactables could be on layer two. And the farmer's reach zone needs its mask set to layer two, so it can see them. If the mask doesn't include the right layer, nothing ever happens, no error, no message, just silence. [frustrated sigh] It's the single most common reason "my Area2D doesn't work". So if you press E and nothing happens, check the layers first.

[calm] A second little gotcha: the overlapping list only updates once per physics step. So it's empty on the very first frame something appears. That almost never matters for a farmer walking up to a radish. But it's worth knowing when something odd happens on the first frame of a scene.

[happy] Then, what happens when you interact? That's the object's job, not the farmer's. Every interactable gets a function called interact. A radish's interact pulls the radish. A door's opens the door. The farmer doesn't need to know the difference. [warm] That's a really nice habit in game code: the thing being used decides what using it means.

[curious] And to tell the rest of the game, use a signal of your own. In Godot four, you write it at the top of the script: signal pulled. When the radish is pulled, it says pulled dot emit. Anything that cares, the inventory, the sound, and later, the radish monster's hidden counter, can connect to that signal and react, without the radish knowing they exist. [sly] The radish just announces it's been pulled. [punch] It has no idea what's listening. [dark] [very sweet, innocent] [speaking slowly] Which is exactly... how it should be.

[happy] Finally, the bit that makes it feel good. When the radish is pulled, don't just delete it. Give it a tiny pop. Godot's tweens make this easy: create tween, then tween property, to scale it up to about one point three for a tenth of a second, with a little bounce, then down to nothing, and then call queue free, which removes the node safely at the end of the frame. [warm] A quarter of a second of animation, and suddenly pulling a radish feels like pulling a radish, not like a sprite blinking out of existence.
