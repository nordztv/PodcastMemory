[calm] Step one: the town. They shot for fifty four days around Lone Pine and Olancha in California, near the Alabama Hills, at the foot of the Sierra Nevada. Perfection was built from scratch near Olancha. That real desert light is a big part of why it feels so solid.

[calm] Step two: the monsters. The creatures were built by Amalgamated Dynamics, the company started by Alec Gillis and Tom Woodruff Junior, who'd come from Stan Winston's shop. [curious] Step three: making them move. In the pit, a full-size head was worked like a giant rod puppet. [calm] Step five: the tongues. Inside the Graboid's mouth are three snake-like tentacles with their own little mouths. Three cable-operated ten-foot tongues were built on a spine of braided hydraulic hose, plus a hand puppet for close-ups, and nine simple stunt tentacles for anything rough. The design borrowed from snakes, elephant trunks and octopus.

[calm] Step six: the miniatures. For wide shots, the Skotak brothers' team used five quarter-scale animatronic Graboids, each needing six to eight operators, plus smaller hand puppets. Big things fall slow. Your brain knows the difference.

[thoughtful] So, the practical lesson. Tremors is mostly a film about something you can't see. The Graboids are underground for most of it.
