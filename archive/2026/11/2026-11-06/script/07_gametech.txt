[happy] Game tech, and it's review day. This week in Lanternfall we've built two big foundations: a farmer who walks, and a world you can reach out and touch. So let's check what "done" looks like, how to test it, and the bugs that usually bite.

[curious] First, what you should have by now. A Player scene, built on a CharacterBody2D, with a small collision shape just at the feet. And a Radish scene: an Area2D on its own collision layer, in a group called interactable, with a script that sends a signal called pulled, and pops out of the ground with a little tween.

[warm] Now, a five-minute test. Think of it like a pilot's checklist before take-off.

[calm] One. Walk in all eight directions. Diagonals should be the same speed as straight lines. If diagonals feel faster, you're probably adding the directions yourself instead of using Input dot get vector, which keeps the length right for you.

[calm] Two. Walk into the fence from every side, and slide along it. If the farmer snags on corners, or gets stuck, the collision shape is usually too big or too square. Shrink it, or round it off with a capsule.

[calm] Three. Walk up to a radish and press interact once. It should pop exactly once. If it pops twice, or the signal fires twice, check that you're using is action just pressed, not is action pressed, which is true for every frame the button's held down. And that little is pulled guard we added on Thursday stops a second press while the tween's still playing.

[calm] Four. Stand between two radishes and press interact. Only one should come up. If both do, you're looping over everything in reach and pulling it all. Pick the nearest one and stop there.

[calm] Five. Press interact standing in an empty field. Nothing should happen, and nothing should crash.

[curious] And now the three bugs that catch almost everyone, so you can spot them fast.

[thoughtful] The silent area. You walk right up to the radish, and nothing. No error, no pull. Nine times out of ten that's collision layers and masks. The radish lives on one layer. The farmer's reach area has to look at that layer, through its mask. If the numbers don't match, they can't see each other. It's like two people waving at each other through a window with the blinds down.

[thoughtful] The ghost radish. You pull a radish, it disappears, and then the game complains about something that no longer exists. That's usually a reference held onto after queue free. Let the radish announce it's leaving, through the pulled signal, and let anything holding onto it let go.

[thoughtful] And the shimmer. Pixel art that wobbles or blurs as the farmer moves. On Wednesday we talked about the texture filter set to nearest, and the viewport stretch setting, in the project settings. Turning on pixel snapping can help too.

[excited] And a stretch goal, if you've got time this weekend. Make the pull feel good. A two-frame pull animation on the farmer. A tiny screen shake, or a little puff of dust where the radish was.
