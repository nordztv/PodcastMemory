Let's set the scene. It's the mid nineteen nineties. Final Fantasy has lived on Nintendo consoles since the beginning, and the series is getting more ambitious with every game. Square wants cinematic storytelling, three D characters, full motion video. And the problem is space. Cartridges were expensive and small. Sony's PlayStation used C Ds, which held vastly more data. So Square made one of the most famous decisions in gaming history and took Final Fantasy to PlayStation. In the end, Final Fantasy Seven shipped on three discs.

The team was large for the time. Hironobu Sakaguchi, the father of the series, produced it. Yoshinori Kitase directed. Tetsuya Nomura designed the characters, which is why everyone suddenly had dramatic hair and a lot of belts. Kazushige Nojima worked on the story, and of course Nobuo Uematsu wrote the music. Square also had artists who had never worked in games before, people from computer graphics and film, working on expensive workstations to build the world.

That world was built in a clever, slightly sneaky way. Most of the locations you walk through are pre rendered backgrounds. Basically, beautiful still images made on powerful computers, with the simple three D characters walking on top of them. The PlayStation couldn't draw a city that detailed in real time, so Square painted the city in advance and let you walk through the painting. It's the same trick a theatre uses with a painted backdrop. It meant the characters were famously chunky, with their little blocky arms, standing in front of gorgeous, detailed scenery. Like Lego figures wandering around inside an oil painting.

Now, Midgar. Midgar is one of the great video game cities, and it works because it tells you the whole story of the game just by existing. It's a huge circular city built on top of giant metal plates, raised high above the ground. The wealthy live up on the plates, in the sunlight. Underneath are the slums, where the sun never reaches. Around the edge are eight Mako reactors, run by the Shinra Electric Power Company. Shinra pumps Mako, the planet's life energy, out of the ground and sells it as electricity. And the game tells you, very early, that this is slowly killing the planet.

So you start the game as Cloud, a mercenary hired by the eco terrorist group Avalanche, led by Barret, to blow up a reactor. Think about how bold that is. The first thing your hero does is a bombing mission for a group the city calls terrorists. The game then spends hours making you ask who the real villains are. It was a very grown up idea for a game in nineteen ninety seven.
