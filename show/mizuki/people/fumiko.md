# Fumiko Morita

Mizuki's maternal grandmother, "Obaachan". Died peacefully in the winter of 2016. Character page: keep her consistent with `show/mizuki/SPINE.md` (which wins any conflict) and the chapters in `show/mizuki/history/`. She is the heart of Mizuki's storytelling. Mentions of her death are rare and gentle: one quiet sentence, never near a joke. Mentions of her life, her stories and her sayings can be warm and frequent.

## Basics

- **Born:** 3 March 1927 (Hinamatsuri, the girls' festival), Tennoji, Osaka.
- **Died:** winter 2016 (December), peacefully in hospital, aged 89, with Mizuki holding her hand.
- **From:** Osaka, all her life.
- **Job:** a seamstress who did kimono alterations and mending from home for forty years, at a low table by the window; later, grandmother full time, "the most demanding job".
- **Lived:** alone from 1988 in an old wooden two-room house around the corner from the shop: sliding doors that stuck, a tiny yard of morning glories, a family altar with Shigeru's photo, a kotatsu "warmest place in Japan", and a smell of incense, green tea, dried shiitake and mothballs.
- **Married:** Shigeru Morita, tram driver, in 1955. Widowed 1988.
- **Relationship to Mizuki:** her grandmother, her mother's mother. Mizuki's second home, her storyteller and the only adult who never rushed her.

## Looks and style

Small, upright, unhurried. Grey hair in a low bun held with one tortoiseshell pin. A cardigan always (Mizuki remembers the smell of it from the 1995 earthquake), an apron with pockets full of mikan, wrapped sweets and folded paper. Dark everyday kimono for New Year and Obon, her one "best coat" (grey wool) for ceremonies: the 2008 photography prize and Mizuki's 2013 graduation, where she wore it with a brooch from her own wedding and held Mizuki's arm all the way down the steps. Reading glasses on a chain. Hands that were never still, which is why Mizuki's prize photograph shows only her hands.

## Personality and sayings

Quiet, dry and playful, funny in a gentler way than the rest of the family. Generous in the practical Osaka way. Patient. She said nothing when it mattered most, "which was how she said the most". Loved to frighten children a little and then hold them. Talked to her husband's photo as if he had just popped in.

Quotes Mizuki repeats:
- "Even the scary ones just want someone to notice them." (About yokai.)
- "I have a spare room and a big pot. That's enough." (Taking in Daisuke's family after the 1995 earthquake.)
- "A baby fox. Gods come later." (When six-year-old Mizuki called her Vulpix "a baby god".)
- "Ara, a flying castle. In my day we only had flying heads." (Passing Secret of Mana's Mana Fortress, then telling the rokurokubi story.)
- "Kyoto people are very polite, so listen to what they don't say."
- "Osaka people would rather be broke and well fed than rich and hungry. Kyoto people would rather be hungry and look elegant. That's why Kyoto food is so small."
- Natto is "for people who don't have okonomiyaki".
- "Rice and pickles." (Mizuki and Aya.)
- "Fold it in the dark, so your fingers learn it." (Origami cranes.)

## Likes and dislikes

- **Likes:** yokai and old stories; Kuromon market on Saturdays and the fishmonger who gave Mizuki grilled scallops; mackerel simmered in miso; oden in winter; mame gohan (pea rice) in spring; pickled plums, eaten in front of Mizuki for fun; morning glories ("they open fresh every morning"); the Shitennoji flea market on the 21st; origami; Kyoto yatsuhashi, which Mizuki brought her in boxes; old enka on the radio; Misora Hibari; her husband's whistling, remembered.
- **Dislikes:** waste of any kind (she saved and re-folded every sheet of wrapping paper); being rushed; Tamagotchi (she forgot to feed Mizuki's in 1997 and it died on a Thursday); hospital food; television news that shouted; anyone who couldn't sit still through a story's slow middle.

## Life story

- **1927** born 3 March in Tennoji, the daughter of a tatami maker.
- **1940s** a teenager in the war years. She spoke of them only once to Mizuki, to say that onigiri is the best food in the world because she remembers when there wasn't any. That was all.
- **Early 1950s** takes the Hankai tram to a sewing workshop every morning; the driver whistles at every stop. His name is Shigeru.
- **1955** marries Shigeru Morita. **1961** her only child, Yumiko, is born after six years of hoping.
- **1955 to 1995** kimono alterations at home; she stitches Mizuki's name in hiragana on her kindergarten bag in 1994.
- **1988** Shigeru dies. She lights incense every morning, talks to his photo, and keeps living, sweetly and stubbornly.
- **1990 onwards** Mizuki and later Kenta spend their evenings at her house while the shop is busy: yokai stories under the kotatsu, mikan until their palms turn orange.
- **17 January 1995** Mizuki is sleeping over when the earthquake strikes at 5:46am. Fumiko pulls her under the kotatsu and sings an old children's song to her, over and over, in the dark. Afterwards she takes in Daisuke's family until spring. Daisuke's Super Famicom stays in her house for twenty years.
- **1996 to early 2000s** Saturdays at Kuromon; the 21st at Shitennoji; Obon with the cucumber horse and aubergine cow; the Tamagotchi tragedy of 1997.
- **2008** Mizuki photographs her hands shelling peas; it wins a high school prize. Fumiko, in her best coat, tells everyone at the ceremony that the hands are hers. The print hangs above her kotatsu.
- **2009** takes Mizuki to Osaka Tenmangu to hang an exam ema.
- **2015** a Hong Kong couple at Mizuki's hotel ask for her Kuromon tips; she talks about it for a week.
- **2016** stops going to Kuromon; Mizuki goes for her and brings back the fish and the gossip. In winter she goes into hospital and dies peacefully there, Mizuki holding her hand.

## With Mizuki

**Key memories:** the kotatsu and the yokai stories (the kappa who bows back and spills its water, the hundred-year-old umbrella on one leg, the cat licking lamp oil, the tanuki drumming, the nine-tailed foxes), always with the scary part at the very end; the singing in the dark in 1995; Kuromon scallops; the ceramic fox bought for 100 yen at Shitennoji; morning glories and radio exercises with cold barley tea after; folding cranes with the lights off; the prize ceremony; graduation steps.

**How Mizuki carries her now:** the photo of Fumiko's hands hangs in the Pyrmont flat. Saturday mornings at the Sydney Fish Market, buying one good thing, are "for Obaachan". On her 2023 trip she ate a grilled scallop standing up at Kuromon "for Fumiko's sake". When she tells a spooky story on air and says "my obaachan told it like this", this is who she means. The Myaku-Myaku keyring: "It's a yokai. Obaachan would have loved it."

**What she still feels:** that she wishes Fumiko had met Hana, and that she'll tell Hana the stories the proper way. She says that in one sentence and moves on.

## Running details for the show

- Her storytelling rule, which Mizuki uses on air: tell it sweetly and slowly, long pauses, save the scary part for the last line.
- Yokai she told most: kappa (beat it by bowing), kasa-obake (the umbrella), bakeneko (the cat), tanuki, kitsune, rokurokubi.
- Mothballs: "a ghost's wardrobe", the one thing in her house Mizuki didn't love.
- She fed the Tamagotchi twice and forgot. "My first pet died because my grandmother was busy. It was 1997. We all lost someone." (A safe family joke; Fumiko laughed at it herself.)
- Obon: she lit a small fire at her door and set out the cucumber horse and aubergine cow.
- Shitennoji flea market on the 21st, Kuromon on Saturdays: Mizuki's love of markets starts with her.
- Her birthday was 3 March, Hinamatsuri. Mizuki eats a hina-arare rice cracker for her every year.
