# Yumiko Tanaka

Mizuki's mother, née Morita. Keep consistent with `show/mizuki/SPINE.md` (which wins) and the chapters in `show/mizuki/history/`.

## Basics

- **Born:** 21 January 1961, Abeno ward, Osaka. Turned 65 in January 2026.
- **From:** Abeno, Osaka. Only child of Shigeru and Fumiko Morita, who grew up in Fumiko's old wooden house around the corner from the shop.
- **Job:** runs the front of the family okonomiyaki shop: the till, the beer, the bookings, the bills, the gossip, the regulars, and the family. Everyone, Hiroshi included, knows she's the real boss.
- **Lives:** in the two rooms above the shop with Hiroshi.
- **Married:** Hiroshi Tanaka, Sunday 8 November 1987 (39th anniversary three days before the first call).
- **Relationship to Mizuki:** her mother. Blood type A, like Mizuki ("which explains everything", she says, about both of them).

## Looks and style

Small, quick and upright, always moving. Short permed hair, dyed a sensible dark brown every six weeks, with one grey streak she calls "my Takarazuka highlight". At the shop: a patterned apron over a cardigan, a pen behind her ear, sleeves pushed up. For Takarazuka: her good navy dress, pearl earrings, and lipstick put on in the train window. She owns one leopard-print scarf, "for emergencies", and is not an obachan, she says, "yet". On video calls she holds the phone too low, so Mizuki mostly sees her chin and the ceiling fan.

## Personality and sayings

A sharp tsukkomi, born to catch Hiroshi's boke and smack him with the menu. Loud, warm, funny, impossible to fool. Remembers every regular's children's names and every rival shop's prices. Practical to the bone. Cries easily and then goes into the back for a minute so nobody sees. Shows love by feeding people and by never letting them get away with anything.

Quotes Mizuki repeats:
- "You're late." (Instead of "welcome home", every time, including March 2023 after five years away.)
- "...And the ochi?" (At the end of any story without a punchline.)
- "Your father is the artist. I'm the one who makes sure the artist gets paid."
- "You smell like a pig's onsen." (To Mizuki after ramen shifts, 2009 to 2013.)
- "It's not." (Deciding in one second that a Kyoto mother's "so lively!" was not a compliment.)
- "Never press it down. Your father will cry." (Okonomiyaki lessons, 2020.)
- "Our Mizuki is going to Australia. With kangaroos." (To every regular, for a month, in 2017.)

## Likes and dislikes

- **Likes:** the Takarazuka Revue above all; karaoke (she is the family's undisputed queen); Matsuda Seiko, the idol of her twenties; a bargain, announced with the price; Toka Ebisu every January; New Year osechi made properly; the M-1 manzai contest every December (she scores the duos before the judges do); her granddaughter; paying by QR code (learned in 2022; very smug).
- **Dislikes:** customers who leave food; Kyoto-style politeness ("say what you mean"); natto, which she once bought "for culture" and has never forgiven; Hiroshi lifting heavy things; silence on a video call; the cost of flights to Sydney; being told to sit down.

## Life story

- **1961** born 21 January, the only child of Fumiko and Shigeru, after six years of waiting. Grows up in the wooden house with the morning glories, riding her father's tram on Sundays.
- **1979** finishes high school and goes to work at the Kintetsu department store at Abenobashi, soon on the gift counter, wrapping New Year and midsummer presents, where she learns to wrap anything and sell anything. Payday karaoke with the staff makes her the karaoke queen.
- **Spring 1985** comes into the Tanaka shop one evening with three women from the department store and sits at the counter. The quiet young cook looks at her, looks at the hotplate, looks at her again, and burns an okonomiyaki for the only time in his career. "Is this a joke?" she says. "It's well done," says Hiroshi. She comes back the next week with her friends, then on her own: "he said three words an hour, and two of them were funny." She learns the Tigers batting order to tease him, and on the October pennant night holds his collar at the Dotonbori canal.
- **1987** marries Hiroshi on 8 November and leaves the department store to run the front of the shop. Her mother-in-law Chiyo, whose till it had been for twenty-three years, comes in "just to help" in early 1988 and stands very close to it, until Yumiko proposes a treaty: Yumiko runs the till, Chiyo keeps the books in pencil, and they go through the figures together over tea. In the early 1990s Chiyo hands her the daily figures and from then on checks the takings every Sunday afternoon. The abacus loses to a calculator.
- **1988** her father Shigeru dies suddenly in his sleep, in the cold part of the year, months after walking her into the wedding hall. She works the front through the weeks afterwards with a brightness the regulars see straight through.
- **1990** Mizuki; **1993** Kenta (born on 22 November, the "good married couple day", which she takes personal credit for).
- **1999** takes Mizuki, nine and just out of her arm cast, to her first Takarazuka show at the Grand Theatre that autumn.
- **2016** her mother Fumiko dies, in winter, with Mizuki holding her hand. Yumiko keeps the altar going in the rooms above the shop.
- **2020** teaches Mizuki the family okonomiyaki by video call, the phone propped against the kettle, after never once writing it down.
- **2023** "You're late", then the back room for a minute. In May she runs the shop's two-week closure like a military operation.
- **2024** becomes a grandmother (Hana, 14 April).

## With Mizuki

**Key memories:** the stool at the end of the counter where tiny Mizuki shouted "irasshaimase"; "...and the ochi?"; the Takarazuka matinee in 1999, the grand staircase finale and the huge white feathers, Mizuki holding her hand so hard it hurt; checking her skirt was rolled once and never twice; dashi powder and okonomiyaki sauce packed into the suitcase in 2018 "in case Australia has no sauce"; crying at the airport gate; the 2020 Sunday-night recipe calls; eleven exclamation marks on the photo of the first shiso leaves.

**Shrine duties:** changes the sakaki on the shop's kamidana on the 1st and 15th of every month (and complains about the florist's prices); "Shinto for life, Buddhism for death" is her phrase. At Kansai in March 2018 she gave Mizuki two charms: a Shitennoji one for her wallet and a Sumiyoshi kōtsū anzen travel charm, which she watched Mizuki tie to her backpack with a double knot; she retied the new ones in 2023 and 2025. Posts a Sumiyoshi ofuda every December. In January 2022 she posted a yakuyoke charm for Mizuki's yakudoshi (33 by old counting) and blamed everything that went wrong that year on it, including Mizuki's COVID ("Imagine without it"); in 2023 she took Mizuki to Sumiyoshi to give thanks it was over.

**How they talk now:** a video call every Sunday morning, Sydney time, with Hiroshi passing behind; LINE messages most days, usually a sticker (she has a Takarazuka sticker set and an Osaka-obachan one) and a photo of something she cooked. They watch Takarazuka DVDs "together" on video call, both talking through the songs. She still opens with "you're late" if Mizuki calls after ten.

**What she worries about:** that her mother works too hard and never says when she's tired; that she's never had a proper holiday that wasn't Shirahama; that Mizuki is the one who isn't there when things go wrong (May 2023 still stings). Mizuki is saving to fly her parents to Sydney, hoped for September 2027. Yumiko has already told the regulars.

## Running details for the calls

- **Takarazuka favourites:** the Revue has five troupes (Flower, Moon, Snow, Star, Cosmos) and the men's roles are played by women, the otokoyaku. Her all-time favourite otokoyaku is **Yuki Amami** (Moon Troupe top star 1993 to 1995, famous for reaching the top faster than anyone, later a big TV actress); "she was too good for one stage". Her favourite show is **The Rose of Versailles** (first staged in 1974), and her favourite character Oscar. She also loves **Elisabeth**, first staged in Japan by Takarazuka in 1996. She cries at "Sumire no Hana Saku Koro" ("When the Violets Bloom"), the Revue's signature song, every single time.
- She takes the Hankyu line from Umeda to Takarazuka with a thermos and onigiri, "because theatre sandwiches are a robbery".
- **Karaoke:** her signature song is Matsuda Seiko's "Akai Sweet Pea" (1982). Her Takarazuka number is the big ballad from Elisabeth, sung with full hand gestures. She has strong views on anyone who picks a song with a long instrumental.
- Every January she takes the new lucky bamboo branch home from Toka Ebisu and hangs it above the till; the old one goes back to the shrine.
- She knows the price of cabbage, every week, and tells Mizuki the Sydney price is "criminal".
- She's always already awake, whatever the hour; up at 5:30 for the market run.
- Hits Hiroshi with the laminated menu, never the paper one ("the laminated one doesn't tear").
- She still calls Australia "the barefoot country", from the family group chat.
