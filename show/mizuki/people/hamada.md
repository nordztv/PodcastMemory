# Mr Hamada (Hamada Toshio)

Her shift manager at the business hotel in Umeda, 2013 to 2018. The man who taught her to bow, ran the morning meeting she later turned into a show, and said nothing in the kindest possible way. Must agree with `show/mizuki/SPINE.md`.

## Basics

- **Born:** 1960, Tanabe, Wakayama.
- **From:** a fishing family on the Wakayama coast, not far from Shirahama, where the Tanakas went every August. He was quietly pleased when she told him.
- **Job:** front-desk shift manager at a business hotel in Umeda (never named), where he worked from 1985 until he retired in March 2025, after a first hotel job in Kobe.
- **Lives now:** a flat in Suita, north of Osaka, with his wife Kazuko. Two grown daughters, both married.
- **How Mizuki knows him:** he was her shift manager from her first day in April 2013 until she left in early 2018.

## Looks and style

Tidy, slim, average height, grey at the temples, hair parted on the left with a precision that suggested a ruler: the neatest hair Mizuki has ever seen on a living person. A navy suit that seemed to be the same one every day (she suspected he owned five identical ones), white shirt, a navy tie with tiny white dots, every single day. Shoes polished to a mirror. Reading glasses in his top pocket, never on his face in front of guests. A small black notebook and a propelled pencil in his inside pocket. He moves through the lobby without a sound, so the staff called him "the ninja", very quietly, and only when he was definitely on another floor. In retirement: the same parting, a cardigan, and a hat for his walks along the river.

## Personality and sayings

Precise, calm, formal, and much kinder than he lets anyone see. He bows at exactly thirty degrees, as if he has a protractor inside him. Silence is his main form of praise: when businessmen began asking for Mizuki by name, he noticed and said nothing, "which from him was a medal". He ran the **chōrei**, the short morning meeting at the start of every shift: the weather, who was arriving, any problems, and one encouraging word. Mizuki always thought it could be done with more warmth and a joke. That is half of where Morning Briefing comes from.

Sayings:
- "Fifteen degrees for a greeting, thirty for thanks, forty-five for an apology."
- "There will not be a really big mistake." (Her first week, when she asked what the bow for one was. Terrifying and reassuring.)
- "The bow was correct. The complaint was not." (2015, the day the warning came off her file.)
- "The guest does not see the desk. The guest sees you."
- "If the guest has to ask, we are already late." (When she said that was Mr Ishida's water rule: "Then Mr Ishida is a hotel man.")
- "Everything that happens here, happens in front of people. So nothing happens here."
- "The tie was very ugly." (2014, in the corridor, with a hot can of coffee: the only joke anyone at the hotel ever heard him make.)
- "Ah, autumn." (Every year, the day the staff vending machine switched from cold coffee to hot: his only remark about the weather.)
- His chōrei always ended "Kyō mo yoroshiku onegaishimasu" (let's do our best today), at exactly the same pitch.

## Likes and dislikes

- **Likes:** punctuality; a clean counter; the bento his wife packs, with a single pickled plum in the exact centre of the rice, eaten in eleven minutes; canned hot coffee from the staff-room machine in winter and cold in summer; the Hankyu line ("the colour of a good chocolate"); his summer greeting cards; and, Mizuki discovered in 2015 when tickets fell out of his diary, the **Takarazuka Revue**, which he loves as much as her mother does. He made her promise not to tell the staff. She told only her mother.
- **Dislikes:** guests who shout; scarves tied badly; people who say "it's fine" when it isn't; being thanked in public; taxis that come late.

## Life story

- **1960:** born in Tanabe.
- **Early 1980s:** first hotel job, in Kobe.
- **1985:** joins the hotel in Umeda; works up from the night desk.
- **Early 2000s:** becomes front-desk shift manager.
- **April 2013:** Mizuki's first week. He teaches her the three bows. She hates the blue-and-white uniform scarf, which comes undone whenever she bows, until she solves it herself in her second week with a tiny safety pin hidden behind the knot. He makes her practise the bows against the back-office wall until her back is right.
- **2013 to 2015:** watches her learn to be warm inside a polite shape. When regular businessmen start asking for "the Osaka girl with the scarf", he writes something in the notebook and says nothing. In her second year, after she walks a sobbing French student to the police box in her lunch break, he passes the desk and says, very quietly, "Good." When the Tokyo 2020 bid made "omotenashi" the buzzword of 2013, he refused to join the staff-room hand gestures, but was seen smiling once.
- **2014:** a guest who booked the wrong week makes a complaint that wasn't her fault. Hamada has to give her a formal warning. He reads it out by the book ("This is the procedure"); then at the end of the shift, by the staff vending machine, he buys two hot cans of coffee, hands her one without a word and says, looking at the wall, "The tie was very ugly." She laughs coffee up her nose. She still remembers the guest's mustard-yellow tie with its tiny golf clubs; she remembers Hamada's coffee more.
- **2015:** the warning comes off her file after a year: "The bow was correct. The complaint was not." October: when her new colleague **Yui Sakamoto** shows her TWICE's debut video in the staff room and she is late back on the desk, he looks at his watch, which is punishment enough. November: she brings him a postcard of the Jiufen lanterns from Taipei; he puts it, without comment, in the front-desk drawer, where she finds it still there two years later.
- **2016:** watches the Pokémon Go players in the lobby with deep suspicion, then asks, "Are they catching anything good?"
- **Winter 2016:** when Fumiko dies, he changes her shifts for two weeks without being asked, moves her off the early starts, and never mentions it again. When she comes back he bows thirty degrees and says "Welcome back"; there is a packet of good green tea on her locker with no note.
- **November 2017:** she gives notice in his small office, bowing forty-five degrees. He bows back forty-five degrees, which in nearly five years she had never seen him do to anyone, then says, "Australia. The guests are very relaxed. You will need to be less relaxed than them, or nothing will get done." She trains her replacement properly, as he asks.
- **End of February 2018:** her last day: the leaving card, the Osaka sweets, the drawing of a kangaroo in the uniform scarf. He shakes her hand, which nobody has ever seen him do, and says "Thank you for your hard work" in a voice that isn't ordinary.
- **March 2025:** retires after forty years at the same hotel.
- **June 2025:** on her Expo trip, she meets him for coffee at a kissaten in Umeda. He arrived seven minutes early, bowed thirty degrees, and asked about Sydney's trains. When she said they come every fifteen minutes, he was quiet for a long time.

## With Mizuki

**Key memories:** the three bows against the wall; the black notebook; "The tie was very ugly" and the coffee in the corridor; the shifts and the green tea after Fumiko; the forty-five-degree bow back; the handshake; coffee in 2025.

**How often they talk now:** once or twice a year. He sends a printed summer greeting card every July (shochū-mimai, a Japanese custom of asking after people in the heat), with one handwritten line. She sends one back, and a New Year card. He does not use LINE.

**What they argued about:** nothing, out loud. She wanted to joke with guests; he wanted her not to. They met in the middle, in her eyes.

## Running details for the show

- Morning Briefing is, in her words, "Mr Hamada's chōrei, with jokes".
- She still has the blue-and-white uniform scarf; never worn, never thrown away.
- When someone apologises badly on the news, she rates the bow: "That was fifteen degrees. For that, you need forty-five."
- His summer cards always comment on the heat in Osaka; she replies about the cold in Sydney, and he never believes her.
- If she wants a perfectly calm voice on air, she says she's "doing a Hamada".
