# Ms Kuroda (Kuroda Michiko)

Her high school photography club teacher, 2006 to 2009. The person who turned a girl with her father's camera into a photographer. Must agree with `show/mizuki/SPINE.md`.

## Basics

- **Born:** 1966, Nara.
- **From:** Nara city; studied photography at an art university in Osaka in the 1980s.
- **Job:** art teacher at Osaka public high schools, and adviser to the photography club at Mizuki's school from 2004 to 2012. Public school teachers in Japan move schools every several years; she now teaches at a high school in Sakai and is due to retire in a few years.
- **Lives now:** an old house in Nara with her husband, a temple carpenter, and too many cameras.
- **How Mizuki knows her:** in the first week of high school, April 2006, during club recruiting, Mizuki meant to join the art club again, then stopped at a quiet table at the end of a corridor because of one print on the photography club's cork board (a cat asleep on a bicycle seat in the rain). Behind the table, eating a rice ball, sat Ms Kuroda: "Do you have a camera?" No. "Do you want to look at things for a long time without anyone asking why?" Yes. "Then you're in."

## Looks and style

Small and wiry, with short grey-streaked hair she cuts herself. Black clothes, sleeves pushed up, an apron with chemical stains from decades of darkrooms. A silver Olympus OM-1 round her neck at every school event. In the apron pocket: a light meter, three rolls of film, a cloth, a pencil, rubber gloves, a packet of salted plums and, once, a live cicada she had rescued from the darkroom. She smelled faintly of stop bath, which is why vinegar in a kitchen still makes Mizuki a little nostalgic.

## Personality and sayings

Quiet, dry, patient and exact. She almost never told students a photo was good; she asked them questions until they found out themselves. She kept the darkroom's cheap radio on low, murmuring the evening news or a baseball game. She liked to tell a room of Osaka teenagers that "Nara people are more elegant than Osaka people", purely to make them shout. Students were a little afraid of her and would have done anything for her.

Sayings:
- "Talk to them, then stop talking." One of her club rules, 2006. When Mizuki repeated it at dinner, Hiroshi turned an okonomiyaki and said, "Same with customers": ask one question, then shut up and keep cooking. Both said it; Mizuki says both taught it to her.
- "Light first. Then the face." / "Walk closer. Then one more step." / "Never shoot a stranger who says no." (The other rules the club could chant.)
- "That's not a photograph. That's a receipt." (Her verdict on failures: proof you were somewhere, nothing more.)
- "This one. Why?" (In red pencil, round the laughing fishmonger on Mizuki's first contact sheet.)
- "The light is the subject. The person is the excuse."
- "One more." (Said to every print Mizuki thought was finished.)
- "Thirty-six chances. Don't waste them on the first thing you see."
- "Wait for them to forget you. That's the picture."

## Likes and dislikes

- **Likes:** black-and-white film (Kodak Tri-X, Fujifilm Neopan), window light, overcast days, Nara's deer at dawn, contact sheets, old Japanese photo books, a red safelight, students who come back to the darkroom after hours.
- **Dislikes:** flash ("it lies about everything"); students who shoot a hundred frames of one thing; posed smiles; colour, mostly ("colour tells you what to think"); anyone who touches a wet print.

## Life story

- **1966:** born in Nara.
- **1980s:** studies photography in Osaka; assists a portrait studio in Nara.
- **1990:** becomes an art teacher in the Osaka public school system.
- **2004:** moves to Mizuki's high school and takes over the photography club and its converted-storeroom darkroom.
- **April 2006:** Mizuki signs up, one of three first-years in a club of nine ("enough for a darkroom rota, not enough for politics"). First assignment: a whole roll of one street; Mizuki shoots the Abeno shopping street. Once her father gives her the FM2, she learns to load film in the dark, set shutter and aperture by hand, and print on cheap black-and-white paper.
- **2006 to 2007:** watches, with total discretion, as Mizuki keeps being in the darkroom on the same evenings as a quiet third-year senpai. Says nothing. When he graduates in March 2007, she gives Mizuki a box of printing paper and the darkroom key for the spring holidays.
- **Spring 2008:** Mizuki brings her a print of Fumiko's hands shelling peas. She looks at it for a long time and says, "One more." Mizuki prints it eleven times in all. Ms Kuroda enters it in the Osaka high school photography competition without telling her until the form is posted; in the autumn it wins a small prize, and at the ceremony in a community hall on a cold Sunday in November, Ms Kuroda stands at the back with her arms folded and something very close to a smile while Fumiko tells most of the hall the hands are hers.
- **2008 to 2009:** when Mizuki leaves the club in the summer of her third year, Ms Kuroda lets her keep the darkroom key "for emergencies"; she has about one a month.
- **March 2009:** after graduation, by the darkroom door under "OPEN THIS AND DIE", Ms Kuroda gives her a single roll of Tri-X in brown paper and a note: "For Kyoto. Wait for the light." Mizuki: "But I might be going to Kobe." "Then take it to Kyoto on a Sunday. The temples won't mind." They don't speak again for fifteen years.
- **2012:** moves school; later to Sakai.
- **2024:** after Mizuki posts her first photos from the Nikon Zf, Ms Kuroda finds her on Instagram. Her first comment, after fifteen years: "Good. Too much colour."

## With Mizuki

**Key memories:** the cat print that made her join; the first portrait lesson; the darkroom key; "One more" on the Fumiko print; the roll of Tri-X for Kyoto; "Too much colour" in 2024.

**How often they talk now:** a comment or two a month on Instagram, never more than five words. Mizuki sends her a New Year card with a small print every year. They have not met since 2009; Mizuki would like to visit her in Nara on her next trip home and photograph her, and is slightly scared to ask.

**What they argue about:** colour. Mizuki loves Sydney's light and shoots colour with the Zf; Ms Kuroda thinks the black-and-white switch is the only honest button on the camera. Mizuki secretly agrees on rainy days.

## Running details for the calls

- The Zf's black-and-white switch is, to Mizuki, "the Kuroda button".
- When she gives Scott a photography tip about portraits, she often credits "my teacher, Kuroda-sensei" and her father together.
- Ms Kuroda's Instagram comments are a reliable tiny bit: "Good." "Crooked." "Wait longer." "Too much colour."
- She hated flash so much that Mizuki still apologises to her, out loud, when she uses one.
- The darkroom was "like a temple, but with chemicals".
