# Mr Kondo (Kondo Shinji)

Owner of the ramen shop at World Square, Mizuki's boss since early 2019, the man who sponsored her work visa and made her shift lead. A Sapporo miso man who hums when he's worried. Never name the shop. Must agree with `show/mizuki/SPINE.md`.

## Basics

- **Born:** 1971, Sapporo, Hokkaido. Turns 55 in 2026.
- **From:** Higashi ward, Sapporo. His father, who worked for the city's snow-clearing department, died years ago; his mother, **Kondo Fusae**, in her eighties, ran the canteen at a local factory and lives alone in the family house in Sapporo. One younger sister, Akiko, a nurse in Asahikawa, two hours away.
- **Job:** owner and head cook of a ramen shop at World Square in Sydney since 2015: Sapporo miso (his pride), a shoyu, and a tonkotsu he makes "because Sydney demanded it", improved with Mizuki's help from 2019.
- **Lives now:** a three-bedroom house in Lane Cove with his wife **Megumi** (also from Sapporo; she does the shop's books and the Saturday till) and their daughter **Rina**, born in Sydney in 2008, now in her first year studying engineering at a Sydney university.
- **How Mizuki knows him:** early in 2019 she saw a "staff wanted" sign in Japanese and English in his window, walked in at three in the afternoon, said "Four years at Ishida-san's tonkotsu shop near Namba, evenings", and he put down his ladle. They argued for forty minutes and he hired her on the spot.

## Looks and style

Solid and cheerful, about 175 cm, round face, a white towel tied round his head in the kitchen, forearms like a sumo wrestler's. Black chef's jacket with the sleeves pushed up, black clogs. He looks older than he is (in 2019 Mizuki guessed he was in his fifties; he was forty-eight, and has not forgiven her). Outside work: a Hokkaido Nippon-Ham Fighters cap, a fleece in every season including summer, and sandals with socks, which Mizuki has photographed as evidence.

## Personality and sayings

Calm, generous, stubborn about soup and soft about people. He runs a calm kitchen and almost never raises his voice; when he's worried, he hums instead. He kept the shop open through 2020 and 2021 and paid everyone what he could. He respects anyone who argues with him well, which is why he hired an Osaka girl. Hokkaido dialect slips out when he's tired: "namara" for "very" (Mizuki pointed out that it's Mr Ishida's "bari" with a parka on) and "shitakke" for "see you".

Sayings:
- "Pig soup for people in a hurry." (Hakata tonkotsu, according to him.)
- "You argue like an Osaka person. The customers will like it." (The day he hired her.)
- "Namara umai." (Very tasty: his highest praise, mostly for his own miso.)
- "Shitakke!" (At the end of every shift.)
- "Nankai mo itta yo ne?" (How many times have I told you?) Said to the soup, never to staff.
- When Mizuki asks for days off for a new Final Fantasy expansion, he sighs and writes it on the roster without a word.

## Likes and dislikes

- **Likes:** Sapporo miso ramen, rich, with butter and sweetcorn; snow, and the Sapporo Snow Festival every February, which he hasn't seen in person since 2014; the Nippon-Ham Fighters (especially the Shohei Ohtani years, 2013 to 2017; he has a framed Ohtani Fighters photo in the staff room); jingisukan, Hokkaido lamb barbecue; Sapporo Classic beer, which you can only buy in Hokkaido; fishing at Balmoral on Sunday mornings; Matsuyama Chiharu, the Hokkaido folk singer.
- **Dislikes:** Sydney "winter" ("it's not winter if nobody dies of it"); people who leave the soup; Hakata noodles ("too thin, like eating fishing line"); late deliveries; being thanked in front of customers; paperwork, which is why Megumi does it.

## Life story

- **1971:** born in Sapporo.
- **1989 to 1997:** after high school, works at a well-known miso ramen shop in Susukino, Sapporo's night district, starting by peeling garlic and ending as the second cook.
- **1998:** marries Megumi.
- **2000 to 2005:** cooks at a Japanese restaurant in Tokyo, which he says taught him that Tokyo people are polite and in a hurry.
- **2006:** moves to Sydney on a sponsored chef's visa to cook at a Japanese restaurant in the city; stays nine years.
- **2008:** Rina is born in Sydney.
- **2014:** last trip home for the Snow Festival.
- **2015:** opens his own ramen shop at World Square with everything they had saved.
- **Early 2019:** hires Mizuki. On her second day she rearranges the water jugs so every table is four steps away; he says nothing and buys two more jugs the next week. Her working holiday visa runs out that year, and he sponsors her for a four-year employer-sponsored work visa, paying the lawyer and never once mentioning the bill. Lily is already working shifts there.
- **2019:** they spend six months improving his tonkotsu. She rates it seven and a half; he expected six. July: approves two days off for Shadowbringers.
- **2020:** takeaway only; cuts hours, pays what he can; works out with Mizuki how to pack broth and noodles separately.
- **2021:** second lockdown, takeaway again. In November, over staff noodles and looking at his bowl, makes Mizuki **shift lead** and full time, and pays her above the award "because you do my job when I'm at the fish market".
- **2022:** in July, when Mizuki has COVID: "Rest. I'll do the roster. Don't come back early to prove you're Japanese." **October:** when Japan reopens, Mizuki cries in his storeroom; he notices, hums the folk song he hums when he's moved, and at closing says, "When you book, give me the dates. I'll do the roster." Then writes the dates in red pen with a small aeroplane, the first drawing anyone has seen him make.
- **2023:** turns the shop TV's sound on for the first time for the World Baseball Classic final (March). Her work visa runs out and he pays for the renewal and never mentions it; she bakes him a castella, badly, and he says "namara umai".
- **2024:** sends Mizuki home from the flu in a taxi he pays for, and sends miso broth. Approves two days off for Dawntrail; just sighs. In October, after her negatives are ruined, puts a hand on her shoulder in the storeroom: "Things, you can lose. The year, you keep."
- **Spring 2026:** in the first week of March his mother goes into hospital in Sapporo. He flies home the next morning and is away more than five weeks, until **Monday 13 April**, ringing most evenings from the hospital corridor for her report; Mizuki runs the shop alone, including on her own birthday ("Happy birthday, shift lead. Close early." She doesn't). She is exhausted; Lily takes extra shifts. Fusae comes home slower but herself, with a cousin checking on her and a carer coming in. He walks back in at ten in the morning with Shiroi Koibito and a box of Royce' chocolate, tastes the tonkotsu, and says "Seven and a half." That week he gives her a bonus he can't really afford, and stops humming for nearly a month. From May he stays open until half past nine on weekdays to make the money back.
- **11 November 2026:** at 11am, for Remembrance Day, he turns the radio down and the whole shop stands still for a minute.

## With Mizuki

**Key memories:** the forty-minute argument and the hiring; the water jugs; the visa he never mentioned; the takeaway lids with Lily's cartoon cat; making her shift lead; the storeroom in 2022 and the red-pen aeroplane; the month she ran his shop, and "seven and a half" after.

**How often they talk now:** every weekday, all day. They eat staff noodles together in the quiet hour after the lunch rush. He calls her "Tanaka" at work and "Mizuki-chan" only when he's about to ask a favour. He sends her a small Christmas bonus every December and pretends it's from Megumi.

**What they argue about:** miso against tonkotsu (permanent; neither will ever win); how long the broth should boil (she says eighteen hours, like Mr Ishida; he says twelve is plenty and the gas bill agrees); whether the shop should open on Sundays (he wants to; she says the staff need a life); and his habit of working when he's sick, which she reports to Megumi.

## Running details for the show

- **The hum code:** he hums when worried, and Mizuki can tell what about by the song. Old enka means the broth (or someone's father); Matsuyama Chiharu's "Ōzora to Daichi no Naka de" means money; a long wandering tune with no end means sums in his head; a low tuneless hum by the stockpot means a late pork order, and a higher one means his mother hasn't answered her phone; a Hokkaido folk song means he's moved and won't say so.
- He calls Mizuki's Final Fantasy days off "the moon holidays".
- He has never met Mizuki's father, but they send each other things through her: Hiroshi sends a bottle of the shop's okonomiyaki sauce every year, and Mr Kondo sends Shiroi Koibito biscuits to Abeno. Hiroshi's only comment so far: "Good biscuit."
- Rina, his daughter, sometimes works the Saturday till in uni holidays and calls Mizuki "the boss's boss".
- When the tonkotsu runs low, he hums all morning: "a bad sign".
- He says Sydney's ramen fans are "kind, but they leave the soup", and he takes it personally.
