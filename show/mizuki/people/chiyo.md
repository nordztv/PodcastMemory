# Chiyo Tanaka

Mizuki's paternal grandmother, Isamu's widow, Hiroshi's mother. Alive, 91, living in a care home in Abeno since 2022. Character page: keep her consistent with `show/mizuki/SPINE.md` (which wins any conflict) and the chapters in `show/mizuki/history/`. Her forgetfulness is told with warmth and humour that she would enjoy herself, never as a tragedy. She is still very much a character.

## Basics

- **Born:** 12 September 1935, Kishiwada, Osaka Prefecture. Turned 91 in September 2026.
- **From:** Kishiwada, the tough little castle town south of Osaka famous for its danjiri festival. She says anyone who grew up watching a danjiri take a corner is not frightened of anything, including Isamu. Abeno from her marriage onwards.
- **Job:** ran the till at the family okonomiyaki shop for twenty-three years (1964 to 1987) and kept the books in pencil in a cloth-bound ledger, totted up on an abacus (soroban), for thirty. Shredded the morning cabbage too. Handed the daily figures to Yumiko in the early 1990s, then checked the takings every Sunday afternoon.
- **Lives:** a care home in Abeno, fifteen minutes from the shop, since 2022. Before that, alone from 2005 to 2022 in the small house with the bonsai, three streets from the shop near the Hankai tram line, that she and Isamu moved into in 1987.
- **Married:** Isamu Tanaka, 1956. Widowed February 2005.
- **Relationship to Mizuki:** her paternal grandmother, "Obaa-chan" on the Tanaka side. Mizuki calls her "Chiyo-baachan" to tell the two grandmothers apart.

## Looks and style

Tiny, sharp-eyed, upright in a wheelchair she uses "for long distances" (anything beyond the dining room). Silver hair set in tight curls every Friday by the home's visiting hairdresser, whom she tips in sweets. A lavender cardigan buttoned to the neck, a brooch shaped like a plum blossom, a little lipstick always. Reading glasses she never wears and a purse she never lets go of. When Mizuki visited in 2025 she was wearing the Australian merino scarf Mizuki sent at Christmas 2023, and told everyone at the home what it cost.

## Personality and sayings

Shrewd, frank, funny, and proud. A little forgetful now about days and names, but still razor-sharp about money: she remembers the price of cabbage in 1974 and will tell you it was a robbery then too. Hiroshi's quietness comes from her side, though she makes up for it in opinions. She and her daughter-in-law Yumiko fought a long, polite war over the till from 1987 and now adore each other, out loud, mostly.

Quotes Mizuki repeats:
- "How much was it?" (Her first question about anything: Mizuki's flights, her flat, her camera, Hana's pram.)
- "Sydney rent? For one room? That's not rent, that's a ransom."
- "Yen is yen. Count it twice." (Her rule from the abacus years.)
- "The abacus never runs out of battery." (To Yumiko, since 1987.)
- "Fifty yen. In 1964 an okonomiyaki was fifty yen, and people complained." (The shop's opening price, as she remembers it.)
- "Your grandfather talked enough for both of us. So I counted."
- "Are you eating?" (Then, before any answer: "You're not eating.")

## Likes and dislikes

- **Likes:** sumo, above all, on the home's television in the afternoons; the March tournament in Osaka (the Haru Basho, held every spring in the city), which she watched in person every year until 2019; Misora Hibari; rice crackers; a good bargain; the home's karaoke afternoon (she sings one Hibari song and then critiques everyone else's); her great-granddaughter Hana; her soroban, which she keeps in her bedside drawer; brooches; being right.
- **Dislikes:** waste; credit cards ("invisible money"); QR codes ("Yumiko's toys"); the price of anything after 1990; noisy residents; the home's miso soup ("they don't use real dashi, I can tell"); being helped before she asks; Tokyo; sumo wrestlers who pull out of a bout early.

## Life story

- **1935** born 12 September in Kishiwada; grows up with the danjiri thundering past every September.
- **1950s** meets Isamu, a loud young cook at a busy okonomiyaki place in the south of Osaka, and starts keeping his money straight. **1956** she marries him.
- **1958** son Hiroshi born.
- **1964** Isamu opens his own shop in Abeno. Chiyo takes the till, the pencil ledger and the abacus, and sets the price at fifty yen. She runs the till for twenty-three years and never once comes up short. When Isamu gives away beer, she writes it down; in 1973 she has two years of toilet paper in before the panic starts.
- **1987** Hiroshi marries Yumiko, who takes over the front and the till. Chiyo and Isamu move to the house with the bonsai, three streets away near the Hankai tram line. In early 1988 Chiyo comes in "just to help" and stands very close to the till, until Yumiko proposes a treaty: Yumiko runs the till, Chiyo keeps the books in pencil, and they go through the figures together over tea.
- **Early 1990s** hands the daily figures to Yumiko altogether; from then on she comes in every Sunday afternoon, in the quiet between lunch and dinner, to check the takings. The abacus loses to a calculator. She has never fully accepted it.
- **1990** Mizuki born. Chiyo gives her a savings book with ten thousand yen in it, "to start".
- **February 2005** Isamu dies suddenly. A few weeks after the funeral she puts his Seiko, in a cloth bundle, into Mizuki's hands. She stays on in the bonsai house, alone, for seventeen years, still coming to the end of the counter every Sunday afternoon to inspect the ledger with her reading glasses on the end of her nose.
- **June 2022** after a fall at home (no broken bones, no lasting harm, though she lay on the floor a while before a neighbour heard her), and some muddling of her days, the family agrees on a care home in Abeno. She moves in with her soroban in the bedside drawer. She chooses it herself, after comparing three on price.
- **March to April 2023** Mizuki's first trip home: Chiyo knows her at once and asks how much the flight cost. Mizuki tells her. Chiyo says nothing for a long time and then, "You should have come by boat."
- **Summer 2024** meets Hana, and asks the price of the pram.
- **June 2025** Mizuki's second visit, with a box of sweets from Sydney. Chiyo, nearly ninety, asks twice where Mizuki lives, calls Australia "that place with the kangaroos and the high prices", and then asks, quite clearly, what Mizuki pays for rent there.

## With Mizuki

**Key memories:** being taught the abacus from seven, on Sunday afternoons at the end of the counter, beads clacking through the takings ("Yen is yen. Count it twice."); the strawberry Mezzo Piano dress and its price; the savings book; Chiyo slipping her an extra otoshidama envelope every New Year "from the till", which Yumiko pretended not to see; Chiyo coming upstairs a few weeks after Isamu's funeral with the Seiko in a cloth bundle: "He said it was to go to you. Because you were always late."; sumo afternoons in the bonsai house with rice crackers.

**Miyamairi:** at Mizuki's first shrine visit, at Sumiyoshi Taisha in May 1990, Chiyo carried the baby in her best kimono, as the paternal grandmother does by custom (Fumiko cried).

**How they talk now:** Mizuki visits on every trip home, always on her first full day. She brings Australian things to be priced: macadamias, a merino scarf, a koala tin of tea. Between trips, Yumiko or Hiroshi hold up a phone during their weekly visit, and Mizuki talks to Chiyo for five minutes; Chiyo mostly asks about money and whether she's eating. Mizuki sends a postcard every month, printed large, which the staff read to her and pin on her wall.

**What she worries about:** that one visit, Chiyo won't know her. Mizuki has decided that if that happens, she'll introduce herself as a girl from Australia with a very expensive flat, "and she'll like me anyway, because she'll want to know the rent".

## Running details for the show

- **Sumo:** six tournaments a year (January, March, May, July, September, November); the March one, the Haru Basho, is in Osaka. Chiyo watches every afternoon of every tournament and phones Hiroshi to complain about the referees. The November tournament (Kyushu) is on now, in November 2026.
- She asks the price of everything. Mizuki prepares her answers before calls, and rounds down.
- The soroban is in her bedside drawer. On good days she still does sums on it faster than Kenta on a calculator, and enjoys that more than anything.
- She calls Australia "the country where coffee costs five hundred yen", and is furious that it's now more.
- Since Mizuki mentioned her cinema friend on a phone call in late 2025, Chiyo asks every time how much a cinema ticket costs in Sydney, and then whether "the cinema friend" paid for his own. (He did. Chiyo approves.)
