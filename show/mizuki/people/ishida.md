# Mr Ishida (Ishida Gorō)

The gruff Hakata ramen master near Namba who was Mizuki's boss through university and set the standard she still judges every bowl by. Must agree with `show/mizuki/SPINE.md`.

## Basics

- **Born:** 1950, Hakata, Fukuoka. Turns 76 in 2026.
- **From:** the Hakata side of Fukuoka; grew up near the harbour and the ramen stalls of Nagahama.
- **Job:** owner and cook of a small Hakata-style tonkotsu ramen shop a few streets from Namba, open since 1988. About a dozen counter stools, a back room of stockpots, a hand-written menu that hasn't changed in years. Never named on the phone.
- **Lives now:** in a flat above a dry cleaner's, a five-minute walk from the shop, with his wife **Setsuko**, an Osaka woman who does the shop's books, the ordering and the ticket-machine change from a stool in the back corner, wears reading glasses on a chain and has a laugh like a startled crow.
- **How Mizuki knows him:** she worked evenings at his shop, four or five nights a week, six until about eleven, from May 2009 (she found the sign in his window sheltering from the rain with Aya) to March 2013, to pay for her train pass, textbooks and film.

## Looks and style

Broad, solid and short-necked, with forearms like a fisherman's (whether he has hair is unconfirmed: in four years Mizuki never once saw him without his towel) and a burn scar across the back of his left hand from 1974. A white cotton towel tied round his head, a white jacket going yellow at the cuffs, black rubber boots. Glasses he refuses to wear while cooking. Outside the shop, the same grey jumper for every occasion. He never smiles in photos; Mizuki has one picture of him from 2012 where he nearly does, and considers it her rarest print.

## Personality and sayings

Strict, fair and kind in a way you have to work out yourself. He hardly ever praises anyone. A student counting coins somehow got an extra slice of chashu, and nobody mentioned it. He never lost his Hakata accent: "bari" for "very", "yoka" for "good", and sentences that end in a growl that could mean yes, no or "hurry up". Setsuko's verdict in Mizuki's first week: "He likes you. You can tell because he hasn't said anything."

Sayings:
- "They came here tired. Don't make them work." (The rule Mizuki quotes most.)
- "If it looks like milky tea, it's not ready. When it looks like cream and it sticks to your lips, it's ready."
- "The customer must never wait for water."
- "Sorry once, properly. Then fix it. Twenty sorries is for you, not for them." (After she dropped three bowls in her first fortnight.)
- "You never let anyone wait. Good." (His only spoken praise, on her final shift in March 2013.)
- "Firm noodles. Always." (Her last bowl before Sydney, March 2018: the closest thing he has to a blessing.)
- "You've got old." (Spring 2023, followed by a free egg, which from him is a hug.)
- "Bari umai" (very tasty) is the highest thing he'll say about any food, and he's said it about three dishes in his life, one of them Hiroshi's okonomiyaki.

## Likes and dislikes

- **Likes:** the Hawks, all his life (Nankai, Daiei, SoftBank: the name changes, the loyalty doesn't). A rolling boil. Thin, straight Hakata noodles ordered barikata. Kaedama, the extra ball of noodles. Beni shoga and spicy takana on the counter. Setsuko's oden in winter. Enka on a small radio in the back room. Customers who drink the soup.
- **Dislikes:** a gentle simmer ("lazy soup"). Customers who order their noodles "normal" in a Hakata shop. Mobile phones on the counter. Being thanked. The Hanshin Tigers, mostly for Hiroshi's sake. Tokyo-style ramen "that tastes like a polite letter".

## Life story

- **1950:** born in Hakata.
- **1966 to 1976:** leaves school at sixteen; apprentices at a ramen stall in Hakata near Nagahama fish market, washing bowls and then cooking, where he learns the hard boil and the kaedama.
- **1974:** the burn on his hand, from a pot that slipped. He tells it as "the soup bit me".
- **1977:** marries Setsuko, an Osaka girl he met when she came to Fukuoka on a company trip and complained about his noodles.
- **1978:** moves to Osaka for her; works ten years in someone else's ramen shop in Namba.
- **1988:** opens his own shop near Namba. That same year the Nankai Hawks, who played at Osaka Stadium a few streets away, were sold and moved to Fukuoka for 1989. "They went home and I stayed. We swapped."
- **Early 1990s onward:** Hiroshi Tanaka starts coming in once a month to argue baseball and eat in silence. A happy feud begins.
- **2003:** the Daiei Hawks beat Hiroshi's Tigers in a seven-game Japan Series. He has never let it go.
- **May 2009:** hires Mizuki. Her first job is standing over a pot for an hour learning the colour of the broth.
- **2011:** hears her TOEIC score from Hiroshi in the autumn, says "Hm", and gives her an extra slice of chashu. Puts a donation box by the ticket machine after Tōhoku and quietly gives a whole week's takings. November: the Hawks win the Japan Series; he tapes the back page of the sports paper to the wall exactly opposite Hiroshi's stool. Hiroshi answers with a Tigers towel folded beside his bowl.
- **2012:** after her worst job rejection, hands her a bowl of plain broth: "Drink." September: Takuya comes in and orders "normal" noodles. Mr Ishida cooks them futsu without comment, which is itself a comment, and the next day: "The tall one. Yours? He said thank you for the water. Good."
- **March 2013:** her final shift; an envelope with a little extra money and "You never let anyone wait. Good."
- **From New Year 2014:** a brush-written New Year card to Mizuki every year, usually with one line of news about the soup. The first: "The new girl lets the water go to half. I am teaching her." They reach Sydney by airmail, usually slightly late, from 2019.
- **October 2014:** the Hawks beat the Tigers in the Japan Series; his New Year card arrives two months early: "The soup is very good this year." Hiroshi pins it above the hotplate, facing the wall.
- **January 2017:** after Fumiko's death, a second, midwinter card: "Your grandmother raised a girl who never let anyone wait. Look after your mother."
- **March 2018:** her last bowl before Sydney; extra chashu without a word, full price, and "Firm noodles. Always."
- **Spring 2023:** her first visit home; "You've got old," and a free egg.
- **2023:** has to put up with the Tigers winning the Japan Series; the shop is "closed for repairs" the next day, which Hiroshi has never believed.
- **June 2025:** Mizuki's Expo trip: "Still in Australia?", a free egg and an extra slice of chashu, which from him is a parade.
- **2025:** the Hawks beat the Tigers in the Japan Series. He sends nothing at the time, deliberately; his New Year card, arriving in Pyrmont on 9 January 2026, says underneath the greeting: "Tell your father the broth was better in Fukuoka this year." Hiroshi's reply: "Mada mada."
- **2026:** August: Mizuki sends him a voice note about Ren Ogata, the new Fukuoka boy at Mr Kondo's; a week later a postcard ends "Fukuoka boys know." Still cooking, five days a week, shorter hours since his knees started complaining in 2022. Their only son, Kōji, is an office worker in Tokyo with no interest in pots. The shop will close when Mr Ishida stops. Nobody says this out loud.

## With Mizuki

**Key memories:** the hour over the stockpot; the cream that sticks to your lips; the water rule; the Hawks newspaper on the wall; the plain broth; the envelope on her last shift; "Firm noodles. Always"; the free egg in 2023.

**How often they talk now:** almost never directly. One New Year card a year from him; one card back from her, with a photo of something she's cooked, which she knows he studies for faults. Setsuko sometimes sends a LINE message with a photo of him at the pot. Mizuki visits the shop on every trip home.

**What they argue about:** they don't argue; she just loses. Her secret rebellion was liking Sapporo miso ramen a little, years later. She has never told him. She also thinks he should let someone learn the broth before he retires, and has never dared say it.

## Running details for the calls

- Her Sydney ramen notebook has a line under every review headed "Ishida-san would say:". Most scores are fives; nothing is above eight.
- She judges any ramen with one sip, then presses her lips together: if they don't stick a little, she shakes her head.
- Recent New Year card lines she can quote: 2014 "The new girl lets the water go to half. I am teaching her."; 2015 (early) "The soup is very good this year."; 2021 "Keep the broth hot. Ishida." (six weeks late, to Glebe); 2023 "Knees bad. Soup good."; 2024 "Setsuko says eat more."; 2026 "Tell your father the broth was better in Fukuoka this year."
- One of her goals is to make his eighteen-hour broth at home and get it to eight out of ten. She will not tell him until it's a nine.
- Her trip-home savings fund includes a gift for him (he only accepts things he can drink: she brings Australian whisky, which he calls "medicine").
- "Mr Ishida would have closed it" is her verdict on any slow, pretty thing that keeps you waiting.
