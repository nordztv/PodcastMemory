# Shigeru Morita

Mizuki's maternal grandfather, Fumiko's husband, Yumiko's father. Died in 1988, two years before Mizuki was born. Character page: keep him consistent with `show/mizuki/SPINE.md` (which wins any conflict) and the chapters in `show/mizuki/history/`. Mizuki never met him; everything she knows comes from Fumiko, Yumiko and one photograph. Keep it warm and light: he is a family legend, not a sorrow.

## Basics

- **Born:** 1924, Sumiyoshi, in the south of Osaka.
- **Died:** in the cold part of early 1988, aged 64, quietly and suddenly at home in the wooden house: he went to bed one night and did not wake up.
- **From:** Sumiyoshi, then Abeno from his marriage onwards.
- **Job:** tram driver on the **Hankai line**, Osaka's last surviving streetcar, which runs from Tennoji and Ebisucho down through Sumiyoshi to Sakai. He drove it for about thirty years, retiring around 1980, when the line was handed to a company of its own.
- **Lived:** with Fumiko in the old wooden two-room house around the corner from what later became Mizuki's family shop.
- **Married:** Fumiko, in 1955.
- **Relationship to Mizuki:** her maternal grandfather, "Ojiichan" on the altar. She knows his face only from the photo and his sound only from a story.

## Looks and style

From the altar photo and the family album: a tall, gentle man with big ears, round cheeks and a wide, crooked smile. In the altar photo he wears his driver's uniform and his tram cap at its slight tilt, standing in front of a green-and-cream tram with one hand on the door rail, squinting into the sun. The cap itself hung on a hook in Fumiko's house. Off duty, a white open-necked shirt, grey trousers, a straw hat in summer and a wristwatch he wound every night at nine. Fumiko said he walked "like a man who was early for everything", because he was.

## Personality and sayings

Cheerful, punctual, a little vain about his whistling. Talked to every passenger. Kind to children and to old ladies with heavy shopping, whom he waited for even when the timetable said not to. A man of routine and small jokes. Fumiko said he was the only person who could make her laugh in the morning.

What the family remembers him saying (Mizuki repeats these as Fumiko told them):
- **He whistled at every stop.** A short, cheerful two-note whistle as the doors opened, as if telling the stop he'd arrived. Regular passengers whistled back. Fumiko first noticed him that way.
- "The tram doesn't hurry, and it always arrives." (His motto, and Fumiko's favourite description of him.)
- "Next stop, Sumiyoshi. Hold on to your hats and your husbands." (A joke he made to the old ladies going to the shrine at New Year, which Fumiko said he got told off for, twice.)
- "Ojiichan was early to everything except his own wedding." (Family legend: he was late because he stopped to help a passenger with a broken geta sandal. Yumiko tells this one.)

## Likes and dislikes

- **Likes:** his tram; whistling; Sumiyoshi Taisha and its steep red drum bridge; Hanshin Tigers on the radio; grilled mackerel; sake warmed in winter, only one cup; Misora Hibari's songs on the radio; the morning glories Fumiko grew, which he watered before work; rain on the tram roof; fireworks at the Tenjin Matsuri; his daughter, completely.
- **Dislikes (as Fumiko told them):** cars that stopped on the tracks; being late; cold tea; people who didn't say thank you to the driver; Tokyo-style ozoni; buses, "which are trams that have lost their way".

## Life story

- **1924** born in Sumiyoshi, close to the shrine and the tram line. As a boy he watches the trams go past and decides, early and permanently.
- **1940s** the war years. He came home in 1946 and, Fumiko said, never talked about them; "he chose to whistle instead". That is all the family says.
- **Late 1940s** joins the tram line as a conductor, then trains as a driver. In those years the line belongs to the Nankai railway company; in 1980 it is spun off as its own company, Hankai Tramway, and he retires around then.
- **Early 1950s** a young seamstress takes his tram to work every morning. He whistles. She pretends not to notice for a long time.
- **1955** marries Fumiko (late, because of the geta). They move into the wooden house in Abeno.
- **1961** Yumiko is born after six years of hoping. On Sunday mornings she rides at the front of his tram on a cushion by the door, watching the rails.
- **1964** the year Isamu Tanaka opens the okonomiyaki shop around the corner. Shigeru is one of its first regulars, two doors from home: modan-yaki and one beer, Fridays.
- **Around 1980** retires. Waters the morning glories, walks to the shrine, rides his old tram as a passenger and critiques the new drivers' braking.
- **1987** on 8 November, aged sixty-three, walks Yumiko into the wedding hall when she marries Hiroshi, the son of his favourite cook. He whistles his two notes when the couple come in, and the whole room laughs.
- **1988** in the cold part of the year, aged sixty-four, goes to bed one night and doesn't wake up. Old regulars who rode his tram whistle the two notes softly to Yumiko when they come into the shop. His photo goes on the family altar. Fumiko talks to it every day for twenty eight years.

## With Mizuki

**What she has of him:** the altar photograph (in his tram cap at its tilt), now on the family altar in the rooms above the shop (it moved there from Fumiko's house in 2016); his tram whistle story, first told and whistled for her, badly, by old Mr Hirano, the retired Hankai conductor on the shop's first stool; a tin tram badge from his uniform, which Yumiko keeps in her jewellery box; and Obon evenings at Fumiko's, watching her grandmother tell his photograph the news "as if he'd just popped in". Those evenings left Mizuki feeling that the dead aren't far away, just quiet.

**How he lives on in the family:** every New Year when she was small, the Tanakas took the Hankai tram from Abeno to Sumiyoshi Taisha for the first shrine visit, and Fumiko made everyone whistle two notes when the doors opened. Mizuki still does it, under her breath, on every trip home. When the Sydney light rail doors open on George Street, she sometimes does it there too.

**What she feels:** curiosity, mostly, and fondness. She wishes she'd heard the whistle. Never sad on the phone; he is a happy story.

## Running details for the calls

- **The whistle:** two rising notes at every stop. Mizuki can do it. She does it, quietly, on the Sydney light rail.
- **Real Hankai facts she's proud of:** it's the last tram line in Osaka; some of its oldest cars, the Mo 161 type, were built in 1928 and still run on the line, among the oldest trams in regular service in Japan. "Ojiichan drove the ones that are still running. Imagine being that reliable."
- Mizuki's hatsumode routine on trips home: Hankai tram to Sumiyoshi Taisha, two whistled notes, the drum bridge (which she still finds terrifying).
- She thinks of him when the George Street light rail is late: "Ojiichan would be ashamed of you."
- "The tram doesn't hurry, and it always arrives": Mizuki's line for anyone who is anxious about being slow.
- He was a regular at the shop before Mizuki's parents met: the families were friends first.
